Two & a Half Millennia

History & Culture

Trace the rise of splendid kingdoms, the coming of Buddhism, and the living traditions that still breathe today.

A Chronicle of the Island

From Ancient Capitals to a Living Culture

543 BC

The Arrival of Prince Vijaya

According to the chronicles, the legendary Prince Vijaya arrived from North India, founding the Sinhala people and the first settlements on the island.

437 BC

Anuradhapura — The First Capital

King Pandukabhaya established Anuradhapura, which would remain the political and religious capital of the island for over a thousand years.

247 BC

The Coming of Buddhism

With the arrival of Mahinda Thera and the sacred Bo sapling, Buddhism was planted on Sri Lankan soil, shaping art, architecture and everyday life forever.

5th Century

Sigiriya — The Lions' Rock

King Kasyapa raised his extraordinary sky-palace atop a giant rock, surrounded by gardens, frescoes and the famous mirror wall.

10th–13th Century

Polonnaruwa — Medieval Splendour

The riverside capital of Kings Vijayabahu and Parakramabahu flourished, famed for its reservoirs and the magnificent Gal Vihara Buddhas.

16th Century

European Encounters

Portuguese, Dutch and later British powers arrived, reshaping the coasts, trade and eventually unifying the island under colonial rule.

1948

Independence

Sri Lanka regained its freedom, carrying forward millennia of tradition into the modern age — the Pearl of the Indian Ocean.

Living Traditions

Culture That Endures

🛕

Faith & Pilgrimage

Buddhist, Hindu, Muslim and Christian traditions live side by side across the island, celebrated in thousands of temples and shrines.

🎨

Art & Architecture

From Sigiriya's frescoes to Kandyan carvings and Dambulla's murals, a rich visual heritage endures.

🍛

Ayurveda & Cuisine

Ancient healing systems and spice-laden flavours — rice, curry, hoppers and tea — are part of daily island life.

🎼

Music & Dance

Drumming and the whirling Kandyan dance carry the stories of kings and gods across generations.
